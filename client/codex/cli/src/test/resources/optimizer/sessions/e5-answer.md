# Why the daytime sky looks blue

Sunlight may look white, but it contains a mixture of colors. As it passes through the atmosphere, tiny air molecules scatter the shorter wavelengths of light more strongly than the longer wavelengths. Blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light, so it is scattered in many directions across the sky. Some of that scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all around us, making the daytime sky usually appear blue.

Near sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through more of the atmosphere before reaching us. Much of the blue light is scattered out of the direct path, leaving more reds and oranges visible near the Sun.
